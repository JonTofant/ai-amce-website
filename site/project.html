About the project

Background

Automation, measurement and control sit behind humanoid robots, reusable rockets, self-driving cars and drones. Many AMC courses still focus on analytical work, with few laboratory exercises and limited tools, and students often do not see how the subject is used in practice.

Since the Bologna reform, students can choose courses across different fields, so groups are often mixed. Teachers usually know little about each student's preparation, especially in the case of international students.

Our approach

The project has two main parts. The first is a set of new exercises from areas such as robotics, transport, agriculture and health. Each exercise has a theoretical, an analytical and an experimental part, at several levels of difficulty.

The second is an AI system that suggests exercises based on the student's prior knowledge, needs and course goal. Teachers use these suggestions as support when they plan the course.

Objectives

  • Make AMC courses more attractive and closer to practice
  • Link theory with simulations, laboratory work and engineering applications
  • Adapt courses to students' prior knowledge, interests and course objectives
  • Help teachers work with mixed groups and identify knowledge gaps early
  • Develop a framework that all partners can use and extend

How exercises are described

So that the AI system can work with them, all exercises are described in the same way, with fields such as learning outcome, difficulty, prerequisites, hardware, software, estimated time and assessment criteria.

The system then compares this information with the student's profile and course goals and suggests suitable exercises.

Work packages

  1. WP1

    Project management

    Coordination, quality assurance, risk and financial monitoring through a Management Board and monthly online meetings.

    Lead: University of Maribor
  2. WP2

    Exercise framework and supported hardware

    A common architecture and template for assignments and experiments, with metadata and shared hardware and software principles.

    Lead: STU Bratislava
  3. WP3

    AI recommendation system

    Pre-knowledge assessment, classification of exercises, the recommendation system itself and a protocol to validate it.

    Lead: TalTech
  4. WP4

    Implementation, validation and testing

    Laboratory models and simulation exercises, tested with students on-site, remotely and virtually.

    Lead: VŠPJ Jihlava
  5. WP5

    Dissemination and training

    Website, webinars, publications, a conference session and outreach to schools and companies.

    Lead: University of Niš

Target groups

  • Bachelor's and master's students in AMC and related engineering programmes
  • International students studying abroad
  • University teachers of AMC subjects
  • Companies and stakeholders in technical sectors
